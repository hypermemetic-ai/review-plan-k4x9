01 · The experience

The organizer connects a calendar once; every review then waits for identity proof.

connect
The meeting organizer connects a Microsoft or Google calendar through an invitation you issue. The connection picks which meetings we join; it proves nobody's identity.
join
A self-announcing, audio-only notetaker joins a supported Zoom or Teams meeting.
leave
They just leave — nothing to start, stop, or upload.
prove
On Teams, Microsoft's directory must resolve the participant to a mailbox in the connected organization. On Zoom, we accept Zoom's own account identity where the meeting exposes it; otherwise the notetaker sends that participant a private message and they verify their inbox — once.
receive
Participants with proven identity get the review link by email, from your domain. Everyone else gets no review, and the ledger records why.
read
On a signed-in device, the link opens the review directly. Otherwise, one click sends a 15-minute sign-in link to their verified inbox — no page ever shows or asks for an email address.

A display name never decides where a review goes.

The wrong person seeing a review is our failure. An unexplained omission is our failure; an explicit no-review outcome with its reason is the safe result when identity evidence falls short. The ledger makes every outcome visible. The other side of the line: we run your review logic exactly as given; judging the coaching stays yours.

03 · Capture

We buy capture from Recall.ai rather than building our own bot.

Recall.ai puts the notetaker into Zoom and Teams meetings and returns the audio and participant records. Deciding who a participant is stays in our own code.

Admission from your calendarsthe connected Microsoft or Google calendars pick the meetings that carry a join URL, up to each meeting's end time
Audio onlyno video is captured; the transcript is built from per-speaker audio streams
What the platforms give usTeams supplies participant identifiers we verify against Microsoft's directory; Zoom join events let the notetaker message one participant privately
Rejoining counts oncewhen the platform shows the same account dropping and rejoining, that is one participant with one outcome

Recordings do not persist: the audio lives only long enough to produce the transcript. We then delete all bot media immediately, and every bot carries a 24-hour automatic deletion fail-safe.

04 · Identity routes

Identity is proven in exactly two ways.

Delivery waits for proof. A display name is never proof.

Microsoft calendar + TeamsAn organization member is matched only when the meeting's calendar belongs to the same Microsoft organization and Microsoft's directory (Graph) resolves that exact participant to a mailbox. Guests, anonymous joiners, personal accounts, and anyone from another organization get no review.
Microsoft or Google calendar + ZoomWhere Zoom exposes the participant's account, that is accepted as proof. Where it doesn't, the notetaker sends that one participant a private message and they verify their inbox once. Names, invitee lists, typed email claims, and generic emailed claim links are never accepted.
Inbox verified onceA participant verifies their inbox once, for all your meetings. You can revoke or delete that enrollment at any time; either takes effect immediately, and no replay of old meeting data can bring it back.
Recognized automatically next timewhen Zoom shows the same account across meetings, the verified participant is recognized without being asked again.
A new private message is not enoughwhen Zoom cannot show a stable account, a fresh private message by itself proves nothing beyond the current meeting. Reconnecting the enrollment also requires continuity proof issued at enrollment — the participant's enrollment credential or a session created by it; a review-viewing sign-in does not count.
No proof, no deliveryWithout that, the participant gets no review — and is not asked to verify again. Every unsupported combination, Google Meet included, ends the same visible way: no review, with the reason recorded.
When proof never arrives

Waiting for proof is time-bounded. If verification is abandoned, expires, is revoked, or fails, that participant's outcome is final: no review, with the reason recorded. Nobody converts that outcome by hand, and nothing falls back to display names.

05 · The link

The emailed link opens the review only on the participant's signed-in device.

Unguessablea 256-bit link; our database stores only a one-way fingerprint, kept separate per purpose, and logs keep only a short prefix
Scanners and forwards see nothingopening the bare link shows a neutral page with no personal data and consumes nothing; Slack and Outlook preview scanners can't burn access, and a forwarded URL reveals no review
One click on a signed-in devicea signed, HttpOnly browser session tied to that one participant within your account opens their review directly
A new device signs in by emailthe only action offered sends a single-use, 15-minute sign-in link to the verified inbox; no page shows or asks for the address
Verification happens oncethe Zoom inbox verification itself signs in the first device; the participant is not asked twice
Sessions end when enrollment endseach valid use extends a session another 90 days; revoking or deleting the enrollment immediately ends its sessions and unused sign-in links
Thirty-day serving windowthe link serves for 30 days from delivery; renewing an expired link is one signed-in click, and never asks for an inbox
Reading is all a session can do

A viewing session opens reviews and nothing else. It can never reconnect an enrollment or change where reviews are sent; those take a separate credential.

06 · Email & infrastructure

Postmark carries the mail from your subdomain; the infrastructure stays small enough to hand over.

The email path

  • A dedicated transactional sender. Each client binding uses one dedicated Postmark server and message stream; review and sign-in emails disable open and link tracking.
  • One exact client domain. The HTTPS review host and visible From domain are the same approved subdomain. A mismatched origin, sender, or server credential fails closed before sending.
  • DNS proves the sender. The client's domain owner adds Postmark DKIM and custom Return-Path records once. Both checks must pass before participant sends are enabled.

The infrastructure

  • One application, one database, one region. A footprint a future contractor reads in an afternoon.
  • Documentation is a deliverable. Architecture notes, a runbook per alert, the test suite.
  • Every account transfers to you whole. Hosting, email, DNS — set up from day one in accounts that hand over as they are.

07 · Risks, named with owners

Four risks shape the design, and each has an owner.

The full register runs 29 entries.

Every participant must consent shared

Operators of this architecture (Otter, Fireflies) are defending class actions; twelve US states require every participant's consent. Our answer is machinery we control: the bot names itself and announces the review's purpose as it joins — every meeting, logged: what was announced, when, to whom. A host script and invite wording exist as offered material, never a required step for anyone. Objections are honored and logged — eject the bot anytime; an individual opt-out means no review, their words excluded. And by architecture, reviews are claimed, never pushed: the email contains no evaluation; reading one is the participant's own act.

Teams can block outside bots from August 2026 ours to detect

Next month, tenant admins get an off-switch for external notetakers. So admission is monitored per meeting from day one: the rate is in your weekly digest, and a blocked counterparty is a same-day report with a visible no-review outcome. We can't join where a tenant says no; we will never be silent about it.

EU rules ban emotion-reading at work ours, by design

Reading emotion from workplace voice is prohibited-practice territory — fines to 7% of turnover. Transcript text is explicitly outside the ban, so the pipeline consumes text only, ever, as a contractual invariant.

What a review says: attribution, quotes, tone yours, informed

Transcripts misattribute and occasionally garble; tone can misfire in a hard meeting. That lives inside the review logic — your side of the line, a trade your design already accepts. Guardrails we recommend and have priced (speaker-count checks, quote verification, a style constitution, a skip-and-flag breaker): separately instructed work, not silently in this contract.

The dependency chain: consent per meeting → tenants admitting the bot → identity-proof coverage → your sender-domain records → vendor continuity → transcript quality → model availability (softest — it queues).

08 · Testing

Every email goes out under your name. The testing is sized to that.

Before first real sends

  • The complete route matrix. Microsoft-calendar Teams and Microsoft-or-Google-calendar Zoom reach only their proof handlers. Every other calendar/platform pair, Google Meet included, ends with no review and a recorded reason.
  • Identity attacks. Duplicate names, renames, invitee claims, organization mismatches, guest and anonymous joiners, colliding accounts, and shared inboxes — none ever makes a name or a claim count as proof.
  • Zoom enrollment lifecycle. The private message, the one-time inbox verification, recognition in later meetings, credential-based reconnection when Zoom shows no stable account, enrollment replacement, revocation, deletion, expiry, and replay are exercised together — including isolation between clients.
  • Controlled live passes on Teams and Zoom. Teams is checked against the connected Microsoft organization. Zoom is checked across first enrollment and later recognition; automated route checks cover both Microsoft and Google calendars.
  • Attacking our own links. Mass guessing, crossed participants and clients, cache and referrer leaks, logs, scanner storms, sign-in link replay, session expiry, and revocation are exercised for real.
  • Placement proven before real mail. Seed inboxes, yours included; re-checked weekly through the pilot.
  • The top-of-hour spike. Meetings end together at :00 — tested at 3–5× peak, retry storms proven unable to double-send.

In production

  • The ledger detects silence. Any row late to its state raises an alarm; the watcher is itself watched.
  • A daily rehearsal. A synthetic meeting runs the whole chain into controlled inboxes; each verified participant must see only their own unique marker, and every unsupported route must keep ending in a recorded no-review.
  • You stay informed without watching. Alerts when something needs attention; a weekly digest of meetings, admission rate, sends, holds with reasons. Clicks counted, not "opens"; aggregates only.

Recommended beyond this contract: the live pass monthly, drills automated, fuzzing to millions of cases.

09 · Build plan

Three stages. 25–30 hours for the initial MVP, capped at 30, inside two part-time weeks.

The core pipeline first — every hour buys delivery integrity before polish. Nothing outside these stages blocks real sends.

11 · Decisions today

Six operating decisions remain; none change how identity is proven.

Each one settled today removes a fork. None widens the two routes or makes a display name count.

How your review logic reaches us

Prompt, rubric, or code — all work; it's held as a versioned template. Same for the page: your design, our serving.

Needed: the handoff form for logic and page design.

The Zoom / Teams mix across your clients

It prices the August Teams change from the Risks slide.

Needed: a rough split, even anecdotal.

Calendar onboarding

A client-issued, single-use invitation authorizes one Microsoft or Google calendar consent flow. The connected account owns scheduling admission; it does not prove organizer or participant identity.

Needed: name the controlled pilot calendars and their client owner.

Expected volume

Meetings per month, roughly — it sizes the load tests and the cost picture. English-first is set; EU exposure is low.

Needed: a number to size against.

Hosting posture

Default: one boring, transferable cloud account we stand up and later hand you whole. If you — or your clients — have a posture we should sit inside, now is the moment.

Needed: inherit or stand alone.

Review host and sender domain

Each client needs one approved HTTPS review origin on the same domain as its visible sender, backed by that client's dedicated Postmark server binding.

Needed: the client subdomain, visible From identity, and monitored Reply-To.

Headed to contract, prepared on our side: the consent split and playbook; retention — bot media deleted after transcript normalization, transcripts and meeting identity 30 days, review serving 30 days, client-retained review until client-directed deletion; account ownership at kickoff.
