01 · The experience

Calendar connection starts capture. Authoritative identity proof decides delivery.

connect
The organizer connects a Microsoft or Google calendar through a client-issued invitation. That connection selects meetings; it does not prove any participant's inbox.
join
A self-announcing, audio-only notetaker joins a supported Zoom or Teams meeting.
leave
They just leave — nothing to start, stop, or upload.
bind
Microsoft-calendar Teams uses tenant-aligned Microsoft Graph proof. Microsoft-or-Google-calendar Zoom uses native authoritative proof when available or a private participant DM followed by one-time inbox verification for client-scoped enrollment.
receive
Only proof-bound participants receive a guarded review link from the client's domain. Every other participant gets a recorded, client-legible non-bound outcome.
read
A valid participant session opens the review directly. A sessionless device requests a 15-minute magic sign-in link at the already-bound inbox; no viewing surface displays or asks for an email address.

Display names never authorize enrollment or delivery. They remain diagnostic input only.

04 · Identity routes

The MVP has exactly two binding route families.

Delivery starts only after authoritative identity proof. Display names never authorize delivery.

Microsoft calendar + TeamsA commercial 8:orgid participant binds only when the connected calendar tenant aligns and Microsoft Graph resolves the exact Teams user_id to a non-null mailbox. Guest, federated, anonymous, personal-Teams, cross-tenant, tenant-mismatched, and unresolvable participants end non-bound.
Microsoft or Google calendar + ZoomZoom-native authoritative proof binds when available; otherwise a private participant-specific DM followed by one-time inbox verification creates a client-scoped enrollment. Names, invitee lists, claimed inboxes, and generic emailed claim links are not proof.
One-time, client-scoped enrollmentInbox control is proved once per participant within one client. The enrollment is revocable and deletable; either action immediately prevents future use, and meeting replay cannot restore it.
Stable-principal recurrenceWhen Zoom exposes an authoritative stable cross-meeting principal, later meetings recognize that client-scoped enrollment automatically.
Meeting-local reconnectionA private DM proves only the addressed endpoint in the current meeting; it never reconnects an enrollment by itself. Reconnection additionally requires a participant-held enrollment credential, enrollment-authenticated session, or equivalent authoritative continuity proof issued at initial enrollment.
No proof, no deliveryWithout valid continuity proof, the participant ends non-bound; inbox verification is not repeated. Every unsupported combination—including Google Meet—ends in a visible, client-legible non-bound outcome.
Hold is terminal

Proof-pending is time-bounded. Abandonment, expiry, revocation, or deterministic proof failure ends in a client-legible non-bound reason; no operator converts that outcome and no path falls back to names.

05 · The link

The review link is guarded by a participant device session.

Unguessable and non-recoverablethe email carries a 256-bit capability; Postgres stores only its purpose-separated verifier and logs keep only a safe prefix
Neutral bare GETa sessionless or scanner-shaped request receives the same zero-PII interstitial and consumes nothing; a forwarded URL reveals no review content
Direct with a valid sessiona signed, HttpOnly device session bound to one participant principal within one client renders that participant's review directly
Magic link on a new devicethe only action sends a single-use, 15-minute sign-in link to the already-bound inbox; the address is never displayed or typed on a viewing surface
One inbox verificationZoom enrollment verification seeds the first device session. The enrolling device opens reviews without another identity prompt.
Bounded and revocabledevice sessions roll for 90 days on valid use; Zoom enrollment revocation or deletion immediately invalidates its sessions and unused sign-in links
Thirty-day serving windowthe review capability serves for 30 days from accepted delivery; renewal is a signed-in action and never asks for an inbox
Purpose separation

A viewing session authorizes review access only. It is never accepted as proof for Zoom enrollment reconnection or endpoint reattachment; enrollment continuity uses a separate credential and verifier purpose.

06 · Email & infrastructure

Postmark carries the mail from your subdomain; the infrastructure stays small enough to hand over.

The email path

  • A dedicated transactional sender. Each client binding uses one dedicated Postmark server and message stream; review and sign-in emails disable open and link tracking.
  • One exact client domain. The HTTPS review host and visible From domain are the same approved subdomain. A mismatched origin, sender, or server credential fails closed before sending.
  • DNS proves the sender. The client's domain owner adds Postmark DKIM and custom Return-Path records once. Both checks must pass before participant sends are enabled.

The infrastructure

  • One application, one database, one region. A footprint a future contractor reads in an afternoon.
  • Documentation is a deliverable. Architecture notes, a runbook per alert, the test suite.
  • Every account transfers to you whole. Hosting, email, DNS — set up from day one in accounts that hand over as they are.

07 · Risks, named with owners

Four risks shape the design, and each has an owner.

The full register runs 29 entries.

Every participant must consent shared

Operators of this architecture (Otter, Fireflies) are defending class actions; twelve US states require every participant's consent. Our answer is machinery we control: the bot names itself and announces the review's purpose as it joins — every meeting, logged: what was announced, when, to whom. A host script and invite wording exist as offered material, never a required step for anyone. Objections are honored and logged — eject the bot anytime; an individual opt-out means no review, their words excluded. And by architecture, reviews are claimed, never pushed: the email contains no evaluation; reading one is the participant's own act.

Teams can block outside bots from August 2026 ours to detect

Next month, tenant admins get an off-switch for external notetakers. So admission is monitored per meeting from day one: the rate is in your weekly digest, and a blocked counterparty is a same-day report with a visible no-review outcome. We can't join where a tenant says no; we will never be silent about it.

EU rules ban emotion-reading at work ours, by design

Reading emotion from workplace voice is prohibited-practice territory — fines to 7% of turnover. Transcript text is explicitly outside the ban, so the pipeline consumes text only, ever, as a contractual invariant.

What a review says: attribution, quotes, tone yours, informed

Transcripts misattribute and occasionally garble; tone can misfire in a hard meeting. That lives inside the review logic — your side of the line, a trade your design already accepts. Guardrails we recommend and have priced (speaker-count checks, quote verification, a style constitution, a skip-and-flag breaker): separately instructed work, not silently in this contract.

The dependency chain: consent per meeting → tenants admitting the bot → authoritative route coverage → your sender-domain records → vendor continuity → transcript quality → model availability (softest — it queues).

08 · Testing

Every email goes out under your name. The testing is sized to that.

Before first real sends

  • The complete route matrix. Microsoft-calendar Teams and Microsoft-or-Google-calendar Zoom reach only their authoritative proof handlers. Every other calendar/platform pair, including Google Meet, ends terminal non-bound.
  • Identity attacks. Duplicate names, renames, invitee claims, tenant mismatch, guest and anonymous participants, principal collisions, and shared inboxes never make a name or claim authoritative.
  • Zoom enrollment lifecycle. Participant-specific DM, one-time inbox proof, cross-client isolation, stable-principal recurrence, meeting-local continuity, replacement, revocation, deletion, expiry, and replay are exercised together.
  • Controlled live passes on Teams and Zoom. Teams is checked against the aligned Microsoft tenant. Zoom is checked across initial enrollment and recurrence; automated route checks cover both Microsoft and Google calendars.
  • Attacking our own links. Mass guessing, crossed principals and clients, cache and referrer leaks, logs, scanner GETs, magic-link replay, session expiry, and revocation are exercised for real.
  • Placement proven before real mail. Seed inboxes, yours included; re-checked weekly through the pilot.
  • The top-of-hour spike. Meetings end together at :00 — tested at 3–5× peak, retry storms proven unable to double-send.

In production

  • The ledger detects silence. Any row late to its state raises an alarm; the watcher is itself watched.
  • A daily rehearsal. A synthetic meeting runs the whole chain into controlled inboxes; each proof-bound participant must render only its own unique marker, and every unsupported route must remain visibly non-bound.
  • You stay informed without watching. Alerts when something needs attention; a weekly digest of meetings, admission rate, sends, holds with reasons. Clicks counted, not "opens"; aggregates only.

Recommended beyond this contract: the live pass monthly, drills automated, fuzzing to millions of cases.

09 · Build plan

Three stages. 25–30 hours for the initial MVP, capped at 30, inside two part-time weeks.

The core pipeline first — every hour buys delivery integrity before polish. Nothing outside these stages blocks real sends.
